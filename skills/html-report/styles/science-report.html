Rules

For a public-facing results page: an experiment report, an eval, a benchmark. Add rules with a date. Do not remove a rule without a note.

Voice

  1. Write for a busy reader with no project context. The first pass must land.
  2. Follow the write-plain rules: active voice, short sentences, no em dashes, no semicolons, no filler. Instructions 20 words max. Descriptions 25 words max.
  3. Define every term of art once, in place or in the Words section. If a reader needs a wiki, a record id, or an internal code name to follow a sentence, rewrite the sentence.
  4. One claim per takeaway card. A card states the fact and the number, nothing else.
  5. Label every number as measured or estimate. Never blend them in one figure.
  6. State what did not work as prominently as what worked. Use a .take.warn card. A report with no warn card reads as marketing.
  7. Put the honest limits (sample size, who wrote the test, what was not measured) in .limits near the verdict, not in a footnote.
  8. Every metric names its direction at first use: "lower is better" or "higher is better".
  9. Report spread when runs repeat: a range, or a 95% CI. One run gets a "one run, no seeds" warn card.
  10. Sources close the page: a footer of links to the records and raw artifacts behind every number.

Page shape (section order)

  1. Header: the question the page answers, as one sentence. Subtitle: what ran, when, how much.
  2. Flow: how the thing works, as a vertical box-arrow diagram. Each box pairs with a one-line "why".
  3. What varied: the compared setups, one row each, same grid, differences highlighted.
  4. Results: bars with a reference line, then the full table, then 3 takeaway cards (at least one warn).
  5. Mechanism (optional): the one step a reader will ask about, as an equation or two panels.
  6. Words: a glossary grid. Plain definitions, two lines each.
  7. Verdict: the chosen outcome in the dashed .next box, with the why, then the limits block.
  8. Outside comparison (optional): our number vs a published number, and why they differ.
  9. Footer: linked sources and records.

Visual rules

  • Light and dark themes via data-theme on <html>, toggle in the header. Test both.
  • Two series colors: --trunk (the thing we back) and --head (the comparison). --good and --bad are verdict colors only, never series colors.
  • The blue-orange pair passes the colorblind checks but sits above the dark-mode lightness band. That is acceptable only because every bar prints its value on the mark. Keep direct value labels. Do not add a third unlabeled series.
  • Bars carry a dashed reference line where "no better than guessing" sits, labeled on the axis.
  • Use a dot rail, not bars, to show a gap between two values on one scale.
  • Tables mark the best cell green, the worst red, and the chosen row with a green wash and a check.
  • Max three takeaway cards per results block. Three columns on desktop, one on mobile.
  • No dual axes. No rainbow palettes. Label points selectively, not every point.

Checks before publishing

  • Zero internal vocabulary (record prefixes, tool names, seat names, project names) outside the footer.
  • Every claim traces to a linked source or a named artifact path.
  • Estimates labeled as estimates, with the assumption next to the number.
  • At least one warn card. Limits block present.
  • grep -nPi '[\x{2013}\x{2014}]|;|\b(leverag|utiliz|robust|seamless)' <file> is clean outside code and CSS.
  • Both themes render. The mobile grid collapses.
